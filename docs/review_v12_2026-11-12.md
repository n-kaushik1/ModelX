# Independent review of framework v12 (12 Nov 2026)

Scope: the brief (`docs/project_instructions.md`), the leaderboard rules (`info.md`, starting kit), the v12 code
(`submission/v12/solution.py`) and the evidence trail in the notes, plus new measurements made today on this
machine (10-core Mac, exact leaderboard versions). Raw outputs of the new measurements are listed in §7.

## 1. Verdict in one paragraph

The modelling is mature and honest: every design choice is backed by paired outer CV, the leakage audit (§3) found
nothing, and v12 is at or near the ceiling that the public data allow. The public leaderboard can no longer tell
the top entries apart (§2), so further public-score chasing has no expected value. The one thing that can still
move the *final* ranking is the private evaluation, and there v12 has a real, measured weakness that nobody has
looked at yet: **on a machine with few cores the time shares cut the search short, so the framework that gets
retrained on the private problems is not the framework that was validated** (§4). Fixing that, plus two small
robustness gaps (§5), is worth more than any modelling change left on the table.

## 2. Where we stand: the public leaderboard is noise at the top

Holdout sizes are hidden; if the development files are the usual 80 %, the holdouts have about 2,500 / 3,000 /
3,750 rows. Bootstrapping holdout-sized samples from development OOF predictions gives the noise of one
leaderboard score (script `noise.py`, 2,000 resamples; the v12 P3 blend gives the same 0.031):

| problem | metric | sd of one holdout score | 90 % range for the *same* model | spread of the current top 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | ROC-AUC | 0.0073 | 0.841 – 0.865 | 0.8612 – 0.8621 (0.0009) |
| P2 | log loss | 0.0082 | 0.496 – 0.523 | 0.5019 – 0.5048 (0.003) |
| P3 | normalized cost | 0.031 | 0.670 – 0.775 | 0.6636 – 0.6763 (0.013) |

On P3 one false negative on the holdout is worth 0.0044 normalized cost and one false positive 0.00044. The gap
between the P3 leader (0.6636) and our v11 build (0.6679) is **one false negative**. Average rank is therefore
decided by luck among the top 6 on all three problems; the brief says exactly this ("do not assume that a small
improvement on the public leaderboard represents a genuine improvement"). Consequences:

- Do not select the final submission by its public P3 score. Select it by paired outer CV (v12 > v11 > v8 on all
  three problems) and by the synthetic-suite regret.
- Upload v12 once, early, so the zip is proven to load and score in the leaderboard environment (that is the only
  thing the leaderboard can still verify for us). Keep at least 6 of the 30 submissions for the last week.
- Expect the public P3 position to wander by several places between builds that are identical in quality.

## 3. Leakage and protocol audit (nothing found)

Checked line by line in `solution.py`:

- Structure detection (interaction pairs, monotone directions) runs on the training rows of each fold, cached by a
  fingerprint of that subset (`_struct_key`), so the OOF rows never influence the constraints they are scored on.
- Redundancy detection (`_find_redundant`) uses no target and only training rows passed to `fit`.
- GAM knots, standardisation, log transforms, category levels and medians are fitted per fold inside the encoders.
- Tuning scores use early stopping on the validation fold (optimistic), but every library member is re-scored on
  fresh folds with a fixed tree count before the blend, calibration and threshold see it.
- The combiner, the Platt map and the threshold are fitted on the fresh-fold OOF predictions; the Platt decision
  is itself nested; the threshold uses out-of-bag bootstrap rounds. Threshold is from development data only.
- The outer-CV protocol (`experiments/v5/outer_fold.py`) fits on 4/5, saves and reloads with joblib and predicts
  raw rows, which is exactly what the leaderboard does; versions are compared on identical fold seeds.

Two mechanisms were checked today with new measurements because they could have hidden a bias:

**Does k-fold bagging shift the calibration?** The deployed predictor averages five fold models, but Platt is
fitted on single-model OOF logits. On the ten saved outer folds (framework 9 libraries, same mechanism;
`bagcal.py`) the Platt slope fitted on OOF logits equals the slope an oracle would fit on the bagged test logits
(P2 1.034 vs 1.048, P3 1.087 vs 1.094, P1 1.100 vs 1.109), and the attainable log-loss gain is 0.0002 or less.
Not an issue. (Side result: the raw convex blend is slightly *under*-confident, which is why Platt helps.)

**Is log loss the right criterion for the P3 combiner?** On the same true held-out folds (`p3comb.py`), weights
chosen by log loss transfer best; weights chosen by the cost itself or by a log loss restricted to the decision
band are worse:

| combiner criterion | held-out normalized cost | held-out log loss |
|---|---|---|
| bagged Caruana on log loss (what v12 does) | **0.7142** | **0.19722** |
| equal weights | 0.7144 | 0.19753 |
| single Caruana on log loss | 0.7176 | 0.19729 |
| Caruana on log loss inside p ∈ (0.03, 0.25) | 0.7197 | 0.19736 |
| Caruana on cost at the Bayes threshold | 0.7222 | 0.19751 |
| best single model | 0.7231 | 0.19757 |

Also re-confirmed today on the v12 P3 fit: the empirical threshold beats the Bayes threshold in only 19 % of
out-of-bag rounds, so 1/11 is kept; the OOF cost curve is flat from 0.08 to 0.12.

## 4. The real risk: the search depends on the grader's machine

`time_budget` (300 s) is described as a safety net, and on the 20–30-core servers used so far it never bound
(P1/P2/P3 fits of 388 / 304 / 417 s). On this machine with `n_jobs=4` (another 4-thread fit running alongside, so
roughly a 2–4-core grader) the same `fit` looks like this:

| problem | fit time | LightGBM configs run (of 32) | XGBoost (of 18) | CatBoost (of 4) | hybrids (of 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | 619 s | 14 | 10 | 3 | 2 |
| P2 | 725 s | 9 | 18 | 2 | 2 |
| P3 | 833 s | 14 | 18 | 3 | **1** |

The cuts are made by `budget_end` (refinements) and by `fam_hard` (which can cut *ladder* rungs). On P3 the
first hybrid alone took 211 s (learning rate 0.01, patience 450, up to 8,000 trees, five folds), so the second
hybrid, the only change that distinguishes v12 from v11, never ran; the P3 library had 13 members instead of 14.
The blend weights still looked normal, so nothing in the logs flags it.

Why this matters more than anything else:

1. The private evaluation *retrains* `solution.py` on unknown hardware, probably with far fewer cores than the
   development servers, and possibly under a time limit (the brief and the leaderboard page say nothing about
   either). Every validation number for v12 (outer CV, synthetic suite) was produced with the full search.
2. On a 2-core grader the tuning would be cut even harder and the fit would still take 15–25 minutes per
   problem; if a time limit exists, a kill means a zero for that problem.
3. The notes' claim that the search is "a fixed number of configurations, so the result does not depend on the
   machine" is only true above roughly 8–10 cores.

Recommended fix (in order of certainty of benefit; all are framework-generic):

- **Never cut ladder rungs.** The ladder is where the measured gains come from (the notes show a 40-trial Optuna
  search ends where the ladder ends); the random refinements add about 0.0003 at most. Remove the `fam_hard`
  break for ladder items, or give the ladder its own generous bound, and let the refinements absorb all of the
  time pressure.
- **Make the expensive members cheaper without changing what they learn.** The zoo log shows the hybrid at
  learning rate 0.02 is within noise of 0.01 (P1 0.85381 vs 0.85373, P2 0.50678 vs 0.50683, P3 0.19672 vs 0.19670)
  at roughly half the trees; the LightGBM lr-0.01 rung costs the same. Cap `_MAX_TREES` for the slow learning
  rates. Cheaper rungs mean the fixed search completes on small machines.
- **Decide the wall-clock contract explicitly.** Either (a) ask the course staff what hardware and time limit
  the private retraining uses (one forum question; the answer changes the right budget), or (b) design for the
  worst plausible case: calibrate `time_budget` from a quick speed probe at the start of `fit` (time one small
  fixed LightGBM fit) so that the planned configuration count fits, with a hard ceiling of about 15 minutes per
  problem on 2 cores. Document the choice in the report.
- **Validate on the grader's scale, not the server's.** Re-run the paired outer CV of the final build with
  `n_jobs=2` and `n_jobs=4` (the `outer_fold.py` protocol already takes `n_jobs`). Whatever is submitted must be
  shown to be the same framework at 2, 4 and 30 cores. Today's runs suggest the truncated search costs little on
  P1/P2 (the blends resemble the server blends), but that is an impression from one fit, not a measurement.
- Optional, bigger: run the five folds of one configuration as parallel single-thread jobs when `n_jobs` is
  small. LightGBM with 4 threads on 10–15k rows is far from 4× faster than one thread, so fold-parallelism would
  speed the search up 2–3× on small machines. More engineering; only if the time contract turns out tight.

## 5. Robustness gaps found by new probes (`probe.py`, 12 cases beyond `stress_test.py`)

All cases fit, pickled, reloaded and predicted correctly, including labels {1, 2}, {−1, +1}, strings with
`positive_label`, booleans, a float array, integer column names, a 2,500-level categorical, an integer-coded
categorical, unusual metadata spellings and a cost objective with FP dearer than FN. Two findings:

- **A numeric column that contains a placeholder token** (every 7th value `"n/a"`) becomes a 2,000-level
  categorical, which the encoders then cap at 30 levels: the information is lost. The coercion rule requires 95 %
  of the non-missing values to parse as numbers. Real private data often has `"?"`, `"-"`, `"NA"` or `"n/a"` in
  numeric columns. Generic fix: coerce when ≥ 80 % parse **and** the non-parsing values are a handful of distinct
  tokens (say ≤ 5), mapping those tokens to NaN; the native encoders already handle NaN.
- **A unique integer identifier with a non-identifier name** (`account_number`) is kept as a numeric feature. It
  is harmless on the public data and hard to drop safely in general (an integer timestamp looks the same), so
  leave it, but note it as a known limitation in the report.

Everything else in the stress test and the probes behaved. The `fit` path for `y` containing NaN would fail at
`astype(int)`; development files will not contain that, so it is not worth touching.

## 6. Modelling: what is left, and what the 2026 state of the art says

Context (Nov 2026): on TabArena the leaders are tabular foundation models (TabPFN-2.5/3, TabICLv2), which the
brief forbids (pretrained models, deep learning). Among permitted methods the benchmark's best is **tuned and
ensembled LightGBM**, with CatBoost the best untuned default and EBM-style additive models well behind on
accuracy; AutoGluon's permitted recipe is a portfolio of boosters bagged by k-fold CV, combined by greedy
(Caruana) ensemble selection, which is what v12 implements, plus structure-aware GAMs that TabArena does not
have. Recent Kaggle tabular winners add nothing permitted here beyond scale (hundreds of seeds and models) and
"trust CV over the leaderboard". A 2026 study of ensembling tabular models finds exactly what the v5 notes
found: logistic stacking sharpens boundaries and ruins log loss, greedy selection is the safe default.

Against that background the remaining levers, with my estimate of their value:

| lever | evidence | expected value |
|---|---|---|
| Fix the search truncation (§4) | measured today | **largest**: it decides whether the validated framework is the one that runs privately |
| Placeholder-token coercion (§5) | measured today | moderate on private data, zero on public |
| Anything on the public CV | v12 vs v11: +0.00005 AUC, −0.0002 log loss, −0.001 cost | below one holdout sd by 10–30×; not worth the risk of a change this late |
| More seeds / fold repeats | notes: < 0.0001 | none |
| Refit GAMs on all rows (v12b) | tie in the framework | none |
| Feature engineering, feature selection, resampling | screened and rejected with nested CV | none |

My recommendation is to freeze the modelling at v12 and spend the remaining time on §4 and §5, then re-validate
(outer CV at 2 and 4 threads, synthetic suite, stress test, probes), build the zip and upload.

## 7. Compliance and report notes

- No dataset-specific rules, names or fingerprints in the code; all thresholds are generic (e.g. GAM only when
  ≤ 60 numeric columns, 30 category levels). The `record_id` pattern is the brief's own identifier name.
- Only pinned libraries; Optuna is listed as allowed but not required. No stored predictions, no internet.
- "Limited hyperparameter tuning": the framework runs roughly 70 configurations × 5 folds. Frame it in the report
  as a complexity ladder with a short refinement, chosen because an Optuna search converged to the same place,
  and state the wall-clock contract from §4.
- The report should contain the holdout-noise table from §2: it shows the leaderboard was treated as the brief
  asks, and it is the honest explanation for any P3 rank.
- Leaderboard hygiene: 3 uploads/day, 30 total, failed uploads count; the entry *shown* at the deadline is the
  final one, and its `solution.py` is what gets retrained.
